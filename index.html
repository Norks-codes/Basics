How to win your mini-league

The principles the tool is built around — distilled from FPL analysts and past champions.

  • Play your rivals, not the world. To win a mini-league you only need to beat the people in it. Watch what your rivals captain and own; if they all have a big differential, you may need it too, or a bold counter-move.
  • Captaincy is the biggest lever. Your captain scores double and swings more points than any single transfer. The 2025/26 champion captained the standout premium (Haaland) in 22 of 38 gameweeks. Consistency beats cleverness — pick the highest-ceiling nailed player with a good fixture.
  • Take hits rarely. A −4 point hit needs to earn ~5–6 points back to be worth it. Three −4s across a run is a 12-point anchor. Bank a free transfer and move with purpose instead of tinkering.
  • Buy form + fixtures, not names. Target players entering an easy run of fixtures who are also in form and nailed to start. That's exactly what the "Projected (5 GW)" score in this tool measures.
  • Chips win seasons — time them. Don't pre-book chips. Use Wildcard on a big fixture swing or when 4+ players need replacing; Triple Captain on a premium in a double gameweek; Bench Boost when all 15 have good fixtures (usually a double gameweek); Free Hit to navigate a blank gameweek or attack a big double.
  • Own the essential engine, differ at the edges. Not owning a rampant, highly-owned captain option is a risk. Take your differentials in the cheaper, rotation-friendly spots — not by leaving out the obvious captain.
  • Minutes are everything. A brilliant player on the bench scores nothing. This tool discounts anyone flagged injured, suspended or doubtful.

Sources: Premier League FPL champion guide, Fantasy Football Scout, Fantasy Football Fix. See the README in the repository for links.

How this tool makes its calls

No black box. Here's exactly what every projection and recommendation is built from.

1 · A player's base scoring rate

We blend recent form with season-long points-per-game so a player is judged on how hot he is and his proven level:

base = 0.65 × form + 0.35 × points-per-game (if a player has no form yet, we use points-per-game alone.)

2 · Fixture difficulty

Each upcoming match is scaled by how hard it is (FPL's own 1–5 difficulty rating). Easy games lift a projection; hard ones cut it:

Difficulty1 (easiest)2345 (hardest)
Multiplier×1.25×1.10×1.00×0.85×0.70

3 · Chance of playing

Minutes are everything, so we discount anyone who might not start: injured / suspended = 0, doubtful = their % chance (or 50% if none is given), fit = full.

4 · The projection

Projected points = base × fixture multiplier × chance of playing, summed across the games in that window (it doubles up in a double gameweek). Proj 5GW is this over the next five matches; Value is Proj 5GW ÷ price.

5 · Turning that into advice

  • Captain = your available player with the highest next-gameweek projection.
  • Transfers = your weakest projected starters vs the best player you can afford in the same position. A move is only flagged "worth a −4 hit" if it gains ~5+ points over 5 gameweeks.
  • Mini-league = the above, then cross-checked against what your rivals own (threats, differentials, safe vs bold captain).

Where this is strong — and its limits

Honest about the edges:

Strengths: fully transparent, always current (auto-updated), and — unlike the pundits — it knows your 32-manager league and plays it directly.
Limits: it uses FPL's official stats (form, fixtures, fitness). It doesn't yet use bookmaker odds or deep expected-goals modelling the way a premium tool like Solio does — so treat projections as a well-reasoned estimate, not a guarantee, and let a clear eye-test overrule a marginal call.
Data: official Fantasy Premier League feed, refreshed automatically every couple of hours by a GitHub Action and read from this site (no proxy). Any other Team ID falls back to a live relay. This is an unofficial personal tool and is not affiliated with the Premier League. Projections are estimates, not guarantees.
